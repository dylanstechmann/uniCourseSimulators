# Visualization and uncertainty communication

A plot is a quantitative claim with scales and encoding choices. Show raw observations where possible, distinguish independent samples from subsamples, label units, and explain uncertainty intervals. Bar charts of means hide distribution shape; a zero baseline matters for comparisons of magnitude. Color should not be the only encoding. For time series, indicate sampling, smoothing, and missing intervals. Visual clarity cannot repair a biased design, but it can expose suspicious structure.
