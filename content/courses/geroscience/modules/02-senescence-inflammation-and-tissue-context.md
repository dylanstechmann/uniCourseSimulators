# Senescence, inflammation, and tissue context

Cellular senescence is a durable growth-arrest state associated with stress responses and context-dependent secretory programs. No single marker uniquely identifies senescent cells across tissues. Proliferation loss, p16/p21 pathways, DNA damage, lysosomal markers, morphology, and secreted factors should be combined with perturbation and time-course evidence. Senescence can suppress tumor formation or support wound repair, so removing cells may help or harm depending on timing, tissue, and intervention selectivity.
