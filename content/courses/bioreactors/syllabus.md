# Bioreactors & Tissue Culture Engineering

**Maturity: partial. Version: 0.4.0.**

Scale cell culture and tissue maturation through mass transfer, shear, process monitoring, control, sterility, and design-of-experiments principles.

## Prerequisites

Cell biology; Statistics; Transport recommended. Structured required, recommended, and concurrent relationships appear in the manifest and remain subject to author review. This package establishes no university prerequisite equivalency.

## Authored outcomes

- Write a cell-culture mass balance.
- Estimate oxygen-transfer and shear constraints.
- Interpret growth kinetics and process data.
- Design controls and acceptance criteria for a reproducible process.

## Assessment and study policy

Every lesson has public formative items (numeric, single-choice, multiple-select, data-interpretation, structured-response and CSV-upload) mapped to its objectives, plus retrieval cards for the review queue; each practice assessment also lists the course outcomes its items assess. Week 7 is a synthetic-data virtual lab and week 14 pairs a lesson on designing a scale-down experiment with the course case and its self-assessment checklist. These are practice activities with unlimited retries and no institutional grade, university credit or transferable credit. Workload has not been measured.

## Current limitations

The schedule above is proposed, not measured, and has not been reviewed by a qualified instructor. There are no graded homework sets, midterm, final or graded project. The four prototype readings remain short. The package cites no new papers: the lessons teach standard first-order relations with synthetic data, and the typical values they quote (power numbers, dimensionless mixing times, saturation concentrations, specific uptake rates) are textbook-level and vary between sources. All cultures, vessels, cell lines and numbers are synthetic; no lesson gives a laboratory protocol, process recipe or medical advice, and nothing here says anything about any real cell line, vessel or process. Qualified human review is required before this course could meet the complete-course standard.
