# Work, energy, and power

Work is W=∫F·dx and changes kinetic energy according to the work-energy theorem. Conservative forces have path-independent work and can be represented by potential energy. Friction and viscous drag dissipate mechanical energy into other forms. Power P=dW/dt=F·v is the rate of energy transfer. In biological systems, mechanical work couples to chemical energy, but efficiency depends on the defined input and output boundaries.
