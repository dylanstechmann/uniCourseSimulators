## Prerequisites

Physics: Electricity & Magnetism; Calculus I recommended. Manifest relationships remain provisional and establish no university prerequisite equivalency.

## Authored outcomes

- Solve DC networks with Kirchhoff and Thevenin methods.
- Analyze first-order frequency response.
- Select an amplifier topology for a sensor signal.
- Quantify noise, loading, and calibration limits.

## Assessment policy

There are 14 readings, 91 public practice items and 48 retrieval cards, plus the preserved case. Each new lesson has six numeric and three conceptual choices. The lab includes CSV summaries, gain/rejection/decay calculations, structured choices and multiple-select interpretation. Eight preserved numeric items require units; new items request bare numbers in explicit scales. Significant figures, dimensions, diagrams, written diagnosis and uncertainty arguments are not automatically scored. Unlimited retries provide study feedback, not course grades. Outcome links still need qualified review.

## Current limitations

Four compact readings remain. One data lab is not a laboratory sequence; graded homework, exams and a cumulative graded project remain absent. Workload, accessibility and circuit quality await review. The lab's known references, hard clamp, valid input range and separately stipulated drift are synthetic assumptions. They establish conditional electrical explanations without unique real interface identification. The preserved Nyquist key is a nominal two-times threshold, not a guarantee of arbitrary-phase recovery at the exact boundary or practical acquisition/filter performance. The package remains partial, unreviewed and formative-only.
