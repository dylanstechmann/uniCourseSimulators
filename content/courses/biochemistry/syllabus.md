# Biochemistry I: Proteins, Enzymes & Metabolism

**Maturity: partial. Version: 0.4.0.**

A mechanistic and quantitative introduction to protein structure, enzyme kinetics, thermodynamics, central metabolism, and pathway regulation.

## Prerequisites

General Chemistry II; Organic Chemistry I recommended. Structured required, recommended, and concurrent relationships appear in the manifest and remain subject to author review. This package establishes no university prerequisite equivalency.

## Authored outcomes

- Interpret protein sequence, structure, and binding models.
- Quantify enzyme saturation and inhibition.
- Track carbon, electrons, and energy through central metabolism.
- Explain pathway control through multiple regulatory layers.

## Assessment and study policy

Every lesson has public formative items (numeric, single-choice, multiple-select, data-interpretation, structured-response and CSV-upload) mapped to its objectives, plus retrieval cards for the review queue; each practice assessment also lists the course outcomes its items assess. Week 7 is a synthetic-data virtual lab and week 14 pairs a lesson on reading a metabolic perturbation with the course case and its self-assessment checklist. These are practice activities with unlimited retries and no institutional grade, university credit or transferable credit. Workload has not been measured.

## Current limitations

The schedule above is proposed, not measured, and has not been reviewed by a qualified instructor. There are no graded homework sets, midterm, final or graded project. The four prototype readings remain short. The package cites no papers: the lessons teach standard relations with synthetic data, and typical pKa values, stoichiometries and ATP yields are textbook-level values that vary between sources. All scenarios and numbers are synthetic; no lesson gives a laboratory protocol or medical advice, and nothing here says anything about any real compound or person. Qualified human review is required before this course could meet the complete-course standard.
