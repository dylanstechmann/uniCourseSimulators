# Fluid mechanics and microvascular flow

The Reynolds number Re=ρUL/μ compares inertial and viscous effects. In microscale devices Re is often small, making viscous forces dominant, but transitions depend on geometry and entrance effects. Fully developed laminar flow in a circular tube follows Poiseuille's law; the r⁴ dependence makes flow highly sensitive to lumen size. Cells, non-Newtonian fluids, compliant walls, and branching networks violate simple assumptions and may require measured or numerical models.
