# Descriptive differences and inferential limits

A group mean is a summary of the observations in that group. For two groups, define the observed difference as the treatment-group sample mean minus the comparison-group sample mean. The sign depends on this order, so state the subtraction direction before interpreting it. If the vehicle group has a mean of 10.0 μM and the compound group has a mean of 14.0 μM, then the observed difference is 14.0 − 10.0 = +4.0 μM. The plus sign means the compound group’s sample mean is higher. It does not mean every treated replicate is higher.

The difference is descriptive: it summarizes the samples that were observed. A population-level effect is an inference beyond those sampled observations. To quantify sampling uncertainty, an analyst needs information about within-group variation, dependence, sample size, and the analysis model. Two means without standard deviations, replicate values, or an uncertainty interval cannot establish whether the difference is statistically distinguishable from sampling variation. A numerical difference can be estimated even when its uncertainty cannot be reconstructed from the supplied summary.

Causal language needs design information too. If units were randomly assigned to treatment, the between-group comparison can estimate an effect of assignment under assumptions about adherence, missingness, and interference. If treatment was chosen by participants or assigned according to baseline condition, confounding may explain some or all of the observed difference. A table of group averages does not reveal how assignment occurred. Therefore, a defensible interpretation reports the observed contrast and names missing evidence rather than claiming a mechanism or cause.

Use a four-step check when reading a compact data summary: (1) identify the observational or randomized unit; (2) calculate the contrast in the stated direction and preserve its unit; (3) ask whether variability and dependence information are present; and (4) check whether allocation supports the causal wording. For the example above, the supported statement is that the compound group’s sample mean is 4.0 μM higher. Without assignment details and replicate-level variability, the summary alone does not establish causation, statistical uncertainty, or the distribution of individual responses. Those conclusions require additional design and data information.

## Plotting replicated measurements

A graph of group summaries is a second representation of the data, not a substitute for the underlying design. Put the manipulated or explanatory quantity on the horizontal axis and the measured outcome on the vertical axis. State both units in the labels. Before plotting a group mean, calculate it from the independent experimental replicates that belong to that group; repeated measurements nested inside one donor do not become independent donors when averaged or graphed.

For the supplied dose-response example, the vehicle readings 4.8, 5.0, and 5.2 have mean 5.0 signal units at 0 μM. The readings 9.8, 10.0, and 10.2 have mean 10.0 at 2 μM; the readings 14.8, 15.0, and 15.2 have mean 15.0 at 4 μM. The plotted summary coordinates are therefore (0, 5), (2, 10), and (4, 15). A dot plot preserves each condition's location on the stated linear axes. Connecting those points would visually suggest values between tested doses, so a connecting line needs a model-based justification and should not be read as measured data.

The mean-only graph compresses each replicate group to one location. It hides the three individual values, within-group spread, and precision of the estimated means. A fuller report could show all observations, a clearly defined uncertainty interval, or both. The uncertainty display must match the intended quantity (for example, standard deviation for spread or a confidence interval for uncertainty in a mean) and the replication structure. The three mean points alone do not test whether a trend is linear, establish a dose-response mechanism, or quantify sampling uncertainty. A plot supports description; causal interpretation still depends on assignment and other design evidence.
