# Misconception catalog: current molecular foundations

This catalog supports the current partial package. Codes are attached to selected practice feedback; the catalog does not claim that every misconception is assessed or that a score diagnoses a learner's reasoning with certainty.

| ID | Likely evidence in a response | Diagnostic prompt | Next learning step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `hydrophobic-effect-is-a-covalent-bond` | Describes nonpolar groups as forming a new bond to water or to one another. | Ask which water interactions are available at the exposed surface and how burial changes solvent organization. | Re-read the solvent/interactions section, then compare a nonpolar surface with a polar surface. |
| `pH-pka-direction-reversed` | Claims that a group is more protonated when pH is above pKa, or swaps the acid and conjugate-base ratio. | Ask the learner to write `[A-]/[HA] = 10^(pH-pKa)` before choosing a state. | Work one example at pH = pKa and one pH unit on either side; check that the fractions sum to one. |
| `chemical-change-directly-proves-phenotype` | Jumps from one substitution to a claim of total protein loss, disease, or universal function. | Ask which intermediate property was measured and which alternative explanation remains. | Separate chemical prediction, molecular measurement, cell phenotype, and organism-level claim. |
| `sequence-variant-determines-phenotype-without-evidence` | Treats a sequence or structure prediction as a direct assay of cellular or clinical outcome. | Ask what abundance, folding, localization, and functional data would be needed. | Build an evidence chain and identify the missing control for each link. |
| `expression-confused-with-specific-activity` | Interprets less total cell activity as catalytic impairment without accounting for protein amount. | Ask whether activity was compared at matched protein concentration. | Contrast total cellular activity with activity per measured amount of purified protein. |
| `single-cell-system-generalized-universally` | Extends one assay result to all cell types, tissues, or organisms. | Ask which populations and conditions were tested and what replication supports generalization. | Rewrite the conclusion to name the measured property, system, and conditions. |
| `protein-mediated-means-primary-active` | Calls any transporter-mediated movement ATP-driven primary active transport. | Ask whether the transporter directly hydrolyzes ATP or couples a second gradient. | Draw the energy source and direction for each coupled substrate. |
| `concentration-equal-at-electrochemical-equilibrium` | Says an ion must have equal concentrations on both sides at equilibrium. | Ask how membrane voltage contributes to the ion's electrochemical potential. | Recalculate one Nernst example and distinguish equilibrium condition from concentration equality. |
| `transport-kinetics-confused-with-equilibrium` | Says a channel changes an ion's equilibrium potential or consumes ATP per ion. | Ask what changes when a pathway opens: the equilibrium condition or the rate of approach? | Compare a passive channel with a directly ATP-coupled pump. |
| `vesicle-traffic-crosses-bilayer` | Describes a vesicle as pulling soluble cargo through a lipid bilayer, or reverses the cytosolic and lumenal faces during fusion. | Ask whether the cargo crosses a membrane or travels inside a vesicle lumen. | Track a lumenal protein and a cytosolic tail through ER, Golgi, vesicle, and plasma-membrane fusion. |
| `signal-deletion-proves-specific-import-step` | Treats loss of protease protection after a signal deletion as proof of one exact receptor, translocon, or recognition defect. | Ask which individual import step was measured rather than inferred from the endpoint. | Use matched synthesis, time-course, interaction, and orthogonal localization measurements to discriminate steps. |
| `localization-assay-proves-function` | Claims that protease protection, fractionation, or colocalization proves molecular activity or causal function. | Ask what the localization assay directly measures and what functional readout is absent. | State the measured compartment evidence first, then design a separate functional or perturbation test. |
| `binding-equals-correct-destination` | Assumes cargo-receptor binding establishes successful vesicle packaging, delivery, or lysosomal function. | Ask which downstream sorting and compartment measurements were collected. | Compare binding, receptor localization, cargo distribution, degradation, and secretion with recovery controls. |
| `kdel-means-physical-immobility` | Treats KDEL as a permanent ER anchor and denies that tagged cargo can transiently leave. | Ask how an escaped molecule could encounter a receptor in a post-ER compartment. | Draw the retrieval cycle and distinguish steady-state enrichment from continuous immobility. |
| `catalyst-changes-equilibrium` | Claims that lowering an activation barrier changes reaction ΔG or shifts the equilibrium constant. | Ask the learner to label the reactant/product free-energy difference separately from the transition-state barrier. | Draw both catalyzed and uncatalyzed pathways on the same reaction-coordinate diagram; then identify whether a separate coupled reaction changes net ΔG. |
| `km-always-equals-binding-affinity` | Treats Km as a universal substrate-binding dissociation constant. | Ask which microscopic rate constants appear in Km for the stated mechanism. | Derive Km = (k-1 + kcat)/k1 and identify the extra rapid-equilibrium condition needed for Km to approach Kd. |
| `rate-change-proves-catalytic-mechanism` | Attributes a changed whole-sample rate to altered kcat without checking enzyme amount or active fraction. | Ask what was normalized and whether soluble active enzyme was measured. | Compare enzyme abundance, folding or active-site amount, assay calibration, and activity per active site. |
| `inhibitor-pattern-proves-binding-site` | Treats a competitive-looking curve as proof that an inhibitor physically occupies the substrate pocket. | Ask what alternative kinetic or assay mechanisms could produce the observed pattern. | Fit multiple substrate and inhibitor concentrations with uncertainty, test detector interference, and add an orthogonal binding or recovery experiment. |
| `late-endpoint-equals-initial-velocity` | Uses a late product amount as a rate without checking time linearity or substrate depletion. | Ask whether product-versus-time data were collected in the reported condition. | Estimate initial slopes from a validated linear interval and compare more than one enzyme concentration. |

| `resolution-equals-magnification` | Treats digital zoom or a larger display as restored optical detail. | Ask which instrument and sampling parameters determine distinguishable object spacing. | Estimate diffraction-limited resolution and sample-plane pixel size separately. |
| `contrast-equals-resolution` | Assumes a brighter or lower-background image necessarily resolves closer objects. | Ask whether nearby peaks can be distinguished at the given spatial scale. | Compare the roles of contrast, signal-to-noise, and point-spread width. |
| `colocalization-proves-binding` | Interprets channel overlap as direct molecular interaction or causal recruitment. | Ask what spatial scale was resolved and whether an interaction or perturbation assay was used. | Bound the optical conclusion and select a suitable orthogonal binding or causal test. |
| `fraction-enrichment-equals-purity` | Calls a fraction pure because it contains the largest share of one marker. | Ask which other compartment markers and recovery values were measured. | Inspect a multi-marker panel and distinguish enrichment, purity, yield, and recovery. |
| `fraction-localization-proves-in-vivo` | Infers intact-cell localization or function from a pellet label alone. | Ask whether homogenization, contamination, and marker recovery were measured. | Pair input-aware fractionation with intact-cell localization and claim-matched perturbation controls. |
| `strand-polarity-reversed` | Writes complementary strands with the same polarity or reverses the template/product direction. | Ask the learner to label each strand's 5′ and 3′ ends before writing complementary bases. | Pair bases first, then write the product antiparallel to the stated template. |
| `lagging-strand-made-3-to-5` | Claims polymerase synthesizes the lagging strand 3′ to 5′. | Ask whether polymerase chemistry changes direction between daughter strands. | Keep synthesis 5′ to 3′ on both strands and use fork geometry to explain Okazaki fragments. |
| `first-generation-band-proves-model` | Treats a single intermediate-density class after one generation as unique evidence for semiconservative replication. | Ask whether the dispersive model also predicts intermediate density after the first round. | Compare predicted molecule classes after a second generation and state what the assay resolves. |
| `semiconservative-fraction-calculated-from-strands` | Counts strand copies as if the question asks for hybrid strands rather than hybrid duplex molecules. | Ask what the denominator names: strands, duplexes, or signal intensity. | Draw the two daughter duplexes from one hybrid molecule and calculate the fraction of duplexes. |
| `proofreading-equals-mismatch-repair` | Uses polymerase proofreading and post-replicative mismatch repair as one process. | Ask when the error is recognized relative to extension and ligation. | Contrast polymerase-associated 3′-end editing with later strand-directed mismatch correction. |
| `all-damage-uses-one-repair-pathway` | Assigns every lesion to a single universal repair pathway. | Ask how lesion size, helix distortion, chemistry, and replication context differ. | Match pathways only as context-dependent candidates and identify the defining lesion feature. |
| `lesion-signal-equals-mutation-rate` | Interprets a lesion-associated signal as a direct count of heritable mutations. | Ask whether the assay measured lesions, repair products, mutations, or viable descendants. | Separate lesion burden, repair outcome, mutation frequency, cell survival, and DNA recovery. |
| `rescue-proves-direct-catalysis` | Treats restoration after factor rescue as proof that the factor directly catalyzes the measured step. | Ask what indirect effects of expression, cell state, or pathway recruitment remain. | State that rescue supports involvement in the tested system, then add direct biochemical and orthogonal tests. |
| `hr-always-perfect-nhej-always-mutagenic` | Describes homologous recombination as always exact and end joining as always mutagenic. | Ask how template availability and end structure affect the outcome in the tested context. | Compare repair substrates, cell-cycle state, processing, and measured sequence outcomes. |
| `histone-mark-proves-cause` | Treats a histone modification associated with a gene as the cause of its transcriptional state. | Ask whether the observation is a mark, accessibility, transcription, or a controlled perturbation. | Compare matched cell states and perturb a defined endogenous regulatory element with a rescue and orthogonal measurements. |
| `open-chromatin-equals-transcribed` | Equates accessible DNA with active transcription. | Ask whether RNA or nascent-transcription evidence was measured at that locus. | Separate accessibility, polymerase occupancy, RNA abundance, and RNA stability as distinct measurements. |
| `enhancer-nearest-gene-assumed` | Assigns an accessible enhancer to the nearest gene without testing a link. | Ask what perturbation or contact evidence connects the element to that gene. | Test element deletion or sequence perturbation and measure candidate target genes in the same cell state. |
| `technical-wells-as-biological-n` | Counts repeated qPCR wells from one preparation as independent cultures. | Ask how many independently grown and processed biological samples were measured. | Summarize culture-level estimates first; use technical wells to assess measurement precision, not biological variability. |
| `chip-input-igg-controls-interchangeable` | Treats input DNA and IgG/mock immunoprecipitation as interchangeable controls. | Ask whether the question concerns recovered starting chromatin, nonspecific pull-down, or locus specificity. | Assign input, IgG/mock, positive/negative loci, and no-template wells their separate control roles. |
| `chip-occupancy-proves-direct-binding` | Interprets enriched cross-linked chromatin as proof that a protein directly contacts a DNA motif. | Ask what cross-linking and immunoprecipitation can capture besides direct base-specific contact. | State occupancy/enrichment narrowly and use orthogonal biochemical or motif-perturbation evidence for direct binding claims. |
| `reporter-proves-endogenous-regulation` | Treats a plasmid reporter difference as proof that an element controls its native gene in chromatin. | Ask whether the endogenous locus was perturbed and the target transcript measured. | Use the reporter as sequence-activity evidence, then test the native element with matched controls and rescue. |
| `mature-rna-abundance-equals-transcription` | Treats a steady-state mature-RNA measurement as a direct transcription-rate measurement. | Ask whether production, processing, decay, recovery, or cell composition could change the measured pool. | Compare nascent or precursor RNA, mature RNA, and decay evidence with assay-matched controls. |
| `rtqpcr-total-rna-resolves-isoforms` | Uses a shared-exon or total-RNA assay to claim a specific splice isoform changed. | Ask which exon junction the primers or reads actually identify. | Pair shared-region signal with junction-specific assays or sequence evidence and report the normalization denominator. |
| `translation-rate-equals-mrna-abundance` | Infers unchanged protein synthesis from unchanged mature-mRNA abundance. | Ask whether ribosome engagement or nascent protein was measured. | Pair target mRNA with a short-pulse protein-synthesis or appropriately controlled ribosome-engagement readout. |
| `protein-abundance-equals-synthesis` | Attributes a steady-state protein change to synthesis without measuring degradation, recovery, or cell state. | Ask which terms appear in `dP/dt = s - k_deg P` and which were observed. | Compare synthesis and turnover measurements, normalize recovery, and state model assumptions. |
| `half-life-measurement-proves-proteasome-route` | Treats a protein decay curve as proof of a ubiquitin-proteasome mechanism. | Ask which pathway component was selectively perturbed and which route-specific controls were measured. | Use a controlled pathway perturbation, orthogonal turnover assay, and rescue before naming the degradation route. |
| `penetrance-means-transmission` | Uses penetrance as the probability of inheriting an allele, or equates allele transmission with phenotype probability. | Ask which probability is unconditional, which is conditional on genotype, and what age/phenotype definition applies. | Multiply explicit transmission and conditional penetrance terms only under a stated model; include noncarrier risk when it is not zero. |
| `one-family-identifies-population-risk` | Treats a small referred pedigree fraction as lifelong penetrance or a population risk estimate. | Ask how the family was ascertained and whether every carrier reached the assessment age. | Record genotype, age, phenotype criteria, follow-up, and independent families; report a descriptive family fraction separately. |
| `association-equals-causation-genetics` | Treats a case-control allele association as proof that the measured base change directly caused the phenotype. | Ask whether ancestry, recruitment, linkage, environment, or phenotype measurement could explain the association. | Replicate with ancestry-aware or family-based designs and test a specific molecular mechanism with controlled perturbation. |
| `variant-prediction-equals-pathogenicity` | Converts a sequence annotation or computational prediction directly into a clinical or organismal conclusion. | Ask which molecular, cellular, segregation, and phenotype measurements are actually available. | Separate variant annotation from functional effect, cellular consequence, organism phenotype, and clinical interpretation. |


| `occupancy-equals-response` | Treats a modeled receptor-binding fraction as the same fraction of maximum cellular response. | Ask which quantity the binding equation measures and which readout the experiment measured. | Compare direct binding or occupancy with a separate operational dose-response curve; state model assumptions and assay limits. |
| `map-is-the-pathway` | Treats a common RTK or GPCR diagram as the complete wiring for every cell and condition. | Ask which node or arrow was directly measured, in which cell and time window. | Test the proposed branch with matched target-engagement, time-course, and orthogonal perturbation evidence. |
| `phosphorylation-proves-fate` | Infers transcription, differentiation, proliferation, or survival directly from one phosphorylation endpoint. | Ask for the downstream outcome, definition, time window, and matched cell-state controls. | Measure the proposed downstream transcript or phenotype separately and restrict the claim to tested conditions. |
| `declining-signal-proves-feedback` | Treats a declining pathway trace as proof of one induced intracellular feedback mechanism. | Compare ligand availability, receptor surface/total abundance, candidate feedback, and viability across time. | Use controlled input maintenance, time-resolved candidate measurements, specific perturbation/rescue, and alternative-explanation controls. |
| `inhibitor-response-proves-target` | Treats an inhibitor-associated readout change as proof of selective action on the proposed target. | Ask whether target engagement was shown and what the solvent, orthogonal, genetic, and rescue controls establish. | Confirm target engagement and use a different controlled intervention or rescue; keep the causal claim assay- and context-bounded. |


| `cytoskeleton-is-static` | Treats the cytoskeleton as a fixed set of equivalent structural rods. | Ask which filament is involved, what it is connected to, and how its assembly changes over time. | Distinguish actin, microtubules, and intermediate filaments; measure dynamics and task-specific readouts. |
| `integrins-only-anchors` | Treats an integrin image or adhesion change as proof of only physical attachment or of a unique signaling pathway. | Separate ligand binding, cytoskeletal linkage, target engagement, and downstream response. | Measure the proposed signal and use matched adhesion controls, orthogonal perturbation, and a bounded outcome assay. |
| `stiffness-alone-determines-fate` | Infers a universal lineage or tissue-repair outcome from a modulus-associated cell readout. | Ask which cells, matrix chemistry, mechanical property, time, and fate marker were actually tested. | Match ligand and cell state, measure matrix mechanics, perturb the proposed route, and directly test the downstream fate. |
| `elastic-modulus-equals-all-mechanics` | Treats one small-strain modulus as a complete description of a biological matrix. | Ask about stress relaxation, creep, anisotropy, hydration, geometry, rate, and remodeling. | Report the measurement protocol and quantify other relevant time-dependent or structural properties. |
| `traction-correlates-proves-force-pathway` | Treats a stiffness-associated traction or localization trend as proof of the molecular pathway that produced it. | Ask which force route was perturbed, whether the target was engaged, and what the assay directly reports. | Use controlled perturbation/rescue and orthogonal force or biochemical readouts while preserving the inference boundary. |

## Limits of this catalog

A selected option can be guessed. Treat these labels as prompts for further practice, not as psychological diagnoses or durable learner traits. Do not infer intent, intelligence, or mastery from a single error. The current deterministic grader awards points for explicit structured choices; it does not read free-form reasoning.
