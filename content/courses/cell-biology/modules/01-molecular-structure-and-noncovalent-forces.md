# Molecular structure and noncovalent forces

Covalent bonds define a molecule's connectivity; hydrogen bonding, electrostatics, van der Waals forces, and the hydrophobic effect help determine its aqueous conformation. Water's high dielectric constant screens charge, but ionic strength changes that screening. A folded protein or assembled membrane is therefore an ensemble of states, not a rigid drawing. Structure-function arguments should identify the interaction that changes and the measurable consequence.
