# Foundations of Cell and Molecular Biology

**Maturity: partial. Version: 0.3.2.**

From molecular structure to cell behavior: build a mechanistic model of how living systems store information, capture energy, and maintain boundaries.

## Prerequisites

High-school biology and chemistry. Structured required, recommended, and concurrent relationships appear in the manifest and remain subject to author review. This package establishes no university prerequisite equivalency.

## Authored outcomes

- Relate macromolecular structure to function.
- Use free-energy and enzyme models to reason about cellular reactions.
- Trace information flow from DNA to phenotype.
- Interpret controls and evidence in a simple molecular experiment.

## Existing units

1. Molecular structure and noncovalent forces
2. Membranes, transport, and compartmentalization
3. Enzymes and cellular energetics
4. Gene expression and experimental logic

## Assessment and study policy

The four units include seven public formative questions, including multiple-select, structured control/evidence selections and a bounded CSV replicate-summary exercise. One question has three authored variants. Each unit includes two retrieval cards. A course case includes a self-assessment checklist. These are practice activities, with unlimited retries and no institutional grade, university credit, or transferable credit. Workload has not been measured.

## Schedule and current limitations

No full-semester calendar or 14-week structure has been authored. No substantive homework sets, laboratories, midterm, final, or graded cumulative project are included. The four short readings preserve useful prototype explanations. They require substantial expansion and qualified human review before this course can meet the complete-course quality standard.
