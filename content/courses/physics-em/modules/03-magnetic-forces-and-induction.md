# Magnetic forces and induction

A moving charge experiences magnetic force qv×B; current-carrying conductors experience a related force. Magnetic forces act perpendicular to velocity and field, changing direction more readily than speed for an isolated charge. Faraday's law states that changing magnetic flux induces an electromotive force. Lenz's law sets the direction to oppose the change in flux. Sensors, motors, MRI coils, and inductive coupling rely on these principles, with geometry and time-varying fields essential.
