# Electrodes, impedance, and measurement

Biopotential measurements sit on a coupled electrode-electrolyte-tissue interface. A simple model includes solution resistance, double-layer capacitance, charge-transfer resistance, and half-cell potential, but real interfaces are distributed and frequency-dependent. High input impedance limits loading; differential amplification rejects common-mode signals only when electrode impedances and leads are well matched. Safety analysis considers current density, charge balance, contact area, and waveform, not voltage alone.
