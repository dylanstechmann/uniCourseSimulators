# Definite integrals and the fundamental theorem

The definite integral ∫ₐᵇ f(x)dx accumulates signed contributions; geometric area is only one interpretation. The fundamental theorem of calculus connects accumulation to antiderivatives and differentiation. If C′(t) is a production-minus-clearance rate, then C(t) = C(0) + ∫₀ᵗ C′(τ)dτ. Units multiply: (mg/L)/min integrated over min yields mg/L. Numerical quadrature is needed when analytic antiderivatives are unavailable or the rate is measured data.
