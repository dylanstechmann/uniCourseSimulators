# Electrochemical stoichiometry, composition and work

## Learning objectives

1. Calculate a cell potential using reduction conventions and a dimensionless reaction quotient.
2. Relate potential, reaction free energy and electron-count scaling.
3. Account for current, charge and a stipulated product current efficiency.

## Write the reaction before the potential

Tabulated reduction potentials describe reactions written as reductions. To obtain a forward cell potential, subtract the anode's reduction potential from the cathode's reduction potential. Do not reverse the anode entry and then subtract its reversed value a second time. Electron accounting identifies the combined reaction and the positive electron number n transferred per mole of that written reaction extent.

For synthetic couples with stipulated reduction potentials +0.40 and −0.10 V, standard cell potential is +0.50 V when the higher-potential couple is reduced. Stipulate a combined two-electron reaction, n=2. With F=96485 C/mol of electrons, the rounded teaching relation ΔG°=−nFE° gives −96.485 kJ/mol of reaction extent. Potential is energy per charge; free energy includes how much charge the equation transfers.

## Composition can reverse direction

For the same written reaction, E=E°−RT ln Q/(nF), with dimensionless activities in Q and the natural logarithm. Take R=8.314 J/(mol·K), T=300 K and Q=100. The composition correction is approximately 0.059522 V, giving E≈0.440478 V. Combining this E with −nF gives current reaction free energy approximately −85.0 kJ/mol. These are stipulated ideal calculations rather than actual electrode measurements.

If Q becomes large enough, the potential can become zero and then negative for the chosen forward reaction. At E=0, ln K=nFE°/(RT), approximately 38.683746 for this model. Reporting ln K avoids a huge exponential and preserves the relation to free energy. A positive standard potential does not guarantee a positive operating potential for every composition.

The quotient must match the balanced reaction. A pure solid in its standard phase may have unit activity, while a dissolved ion needs the selected activity convention. Concentration alone is an approximation in nonideal solutions. The preserved redox lesson also uses a transformed biological standard convention; a prime or an imposed pH must not be silently interchanged with an untransformed chemical standard potential.

## Scaling is different for potential

Multiplying every coefficient by two doubles n and ΔG° and squares Q and K. It leaves E° and E unchanged for the same physical comparison. In the Nernst correction, the doubled logarithm is divided by the doubled electron count, so the factors cancel. Adding arbitrary potentials as though they were reaction enthalpies is therefore incorrect.

Reversing the entire cell reaction reverses E and ΔG while keeping a positive electron-count magnitude for the reversed equation. Swapping which half-reaction is the cathode is a physical redefinition of the forward direction, not merely a different display preference. Check that the predicted direction and free-energy sign agree after every such change.

## Charge is an amount balance

A current I maintained for a time t transfers charge q=It when the sign and current direction are specified. For a stipulated magnitude 0.50 A over 120 s, the transferred charge magnitude is 60 C. Dividing by F gives approximately 6.21858 × 10⁻⁴ mol of electrons. If the target product requires two electrons per entity, the ideal product amount is q/(2F), approximately 3.10929 × 10⁻⁴ mol.

Those amounts assume all transferred charge serves the specified reaction. With an explicitly stipulated target current efficiency of 80%, target product amount is 0.8q/(2F), approximately 2.48743 × 10⁻⁴ mol. Current efficiency is not the same as energy efficiency or recovered mass yield. A side reaction can consume charge without making the target; product loss can alter recovery after charge has already been used.

The potential used for an ideal reversible energy comparison also differs from a measured operating terminal voltage under load. If 60 C moved at a constant stipulated 0.50 V, electrical energy magnitude would be 30 J. A real system can involve resistance, polarization, time-dependent voltage and energy dissipation. The simple charge balance neither designs an electrolysis apparatus nor specifies safe electrical or chemical conditions.

## Boundaries of inference

An equilibrium potential constrains thermodynamic direction under the selected activity and reaction convention. It does not specify exchange currents, reaction barriers, mass-transfer limitations or electrode degradation. Observing current also does not prove the identity of every product. Product-sensitive information and a complete charge/material balance would be required for a real mechanism or selectivity claim.

Concentration errors, temperature changes and junction effects can all alter measured voltages. An agreement with a synthetic Nernst calculation establishes internal consistency of that model, while a mismatch does not identify a unique cause. The lesson uses hypothetical couples rather than a recommendation about a battery, reagent or biological metal center.

## Worked example

Subtract the stipulated reduction values for E°=0.50 V. Form −2×96485×0.50 and convert joules to kilojoules for ΔG°. At Q=100, subtract the natural-log composition term for E≈0.440478. Double the whole equation and verify that potential stays fixed while reaction free energy doubles. Separately, use 0.50×120=60 C, divide by 2F and apply the stated 80% current efficiency to calculate target amount.

## Common mistakes

Do not multiply a standard potential by a reaction coefficient. Do not use a dimensional concentration product as a thermodynamic quotient without normalization. Distinguish charge efficiency, energy efficiency and recovery yield.

## Limits of this lesson

All potentials, currents and efficiencies here are synthetic teaching values, with no apparatus or chemical handling procedure. Original instruction has substantial AI assistance. The package remains partial, unreviewed and formative-only.
