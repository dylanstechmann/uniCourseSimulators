# Elimination, alkenes, and stereochemical control

E2 elimination is concerted and requires an antiperiplanar arrangement of the C–H and C–leaving-group bonds. E1 passes through a carbocation and can rearrange. Base strength, substrate substitution, solvent, and temperature influence competition with substitution. Zaitsev product is common when the more substituted alkene is more stable, while bulky bases or conformational constraints can alter selectivity. Product prediction should report stereochemistry when geometry constrains the elimination.
