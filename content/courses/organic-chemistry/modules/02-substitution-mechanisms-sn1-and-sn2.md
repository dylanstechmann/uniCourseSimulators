# Substitution mechanisms: SN1 and SN2

SN2 is a concerted backside displacement; its rate depends on both substrate and nucleophile, and a stereogenic center inverts configuration. SN1 proceeds through a carbocation intermediate; the rate-limiting ionization depends mainly on substrate, and rearrangements or partial racemization may occur. Strong nucleophiles, steric accessibility, leaving-group ability, and solvent all matter. Biological nucleophilic substitutions are often organized by enzyme binding and proton transfer, so solution heuristics need context.
