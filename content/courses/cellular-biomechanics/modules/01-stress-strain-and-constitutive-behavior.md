# Stress, strain, and constitutive behavior

Stress is force per area and strain is normalized deformation; neither is a material property by itself. A constitutive law maps deformation history to stress. Linear elasticity is useful at small strain and specified directions, while viscoelastic models include time-dependent relaxation and creep. Biological tissues can be anisotropic, nonlinear, hydrated, and actively remodeled. Compare mechanical data only when geometry, loading mode, temperature, hydration, preconditioning, and strain rate are reported.
