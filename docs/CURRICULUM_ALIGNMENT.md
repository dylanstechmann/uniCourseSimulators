# Curriculum alignment and quality guide

**Review basis:** official MIT OpenCourseWare syllabi and course-resource pages accessed October 5, 2026. Links point to source course pages; Lattice does not reproduce their lecture text, problem statements, or solution keys.

## Design standard

The course map is built as a prerequisite chain rather than a topic collection. Each course defines outcomes, assumed prior knowledge, a four-unit scope, lesson checks, and an integrative case. The course set covers the standard quantitative and science foundation first, then uses those foundations in bioengineering, robotics, tissue engineering, and aging biology.

Quality checks used when authoring the release:

1. **Comparable scope:** course outcomes and topic groupings were checked against the OCW descriptions, prerequisites, and syllabus headings below.
2. **Mechanistic explanation:** notes connect a governing principle to an interpretable biological or engineering consequence.
3. **Assessment alignment:** each unit has a graded check that exercises its stated objective; numerical items specify tolerances and units, and explanatory feedback identifies the reasoning step.
4. **Transfer:** each course ends with an open-response case and criteria that make assumptions, evidence, and limits visible.
5. **Scientific limits:** examples state assumptions where a common model is a simplification, including ideal dilute solutions, linear mechanics, first-order kinetics, independent samples, and simple transport geometries.
6. **Originality and attribution:** OCW pages are curriculum comparators and further-study links, not a source from which course text or graded questions are copied.

## Coverage limits and review plan

The catalog intentionally spans all requested prerequisite areas, but its four units per course are a compact launch core. Before calling any course a full semester replacement, expand it with a week-by-week syllabus, multiple problem-set items per objective, lab/data exercises, cumulative exams, and feedback from subject-matter instructors. The advanced geroscience, physiology, and tissue-culture sequences need especially careful specialist review because direct OCW comparators are broader or adjacent rather than one-to-one matches.

The MIT OCW courses above commonly include multiple lectures, problem sets, exams, and/or projects. Lattice provides immediate feedback for its own practice items and points learners to the source courses for further depth; its local score is formative and is not an MIT score, institutional grade, or credit award.
